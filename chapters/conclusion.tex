\chapter{Conclusion}

This report describes the three parts of the energy-aware pipeline built during this internship, following how data flows through them.

\textbf{JouleQuest} automated a manual and fragile measurement process. It uses a single command per experiment, adaptive burst sizing, and clock synchronization between the PC and the board. Its campaign launcher can measure a full matrix of layers over several days, picking up right where it left off. The result is a set of energy lookup tables for layers like Linear, Conv, and Attention, all measured on real hardware at batch size 1.

\textbf{JouleGrad} makes these tables usable inside a training loop. It provides differentiable energy estimates and clear rules for handling inputs outside the measured regions, avoiding silent errors or zero-cost layers.

\textbf{JouleNAS} closes the loop by using the measured energy as a penalty during neural architecture search (for SimpleConv and ResNet-18). This ensures the discovered architectures are optimized for their actual energy cost on the target board.

For ResNet-18, \textbf{The Bridge} demonstrated physical hardware validation on real silicon, confirming real-world energy reductions of up to $60.1\%$ on the Raspberry~Pi~5 and $20.0\%$ on the NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin while preserving baseline accuracy.

The main lesson of this work is also its biggest open challenge: the energy of a full network is almost, but not exactly, the sum of its parts. The difference (up to $25\%$ on the Raspberry~Pi~5) points to cache effects that isolated measurements miss. Section~\ref{sec:sum-of-parts} presents the evidence and next steps, and Chapter~\ref{chap:future_work} lays out tasks for each part of the pipeline. The tools, measurements, and open questions are all ready for the next person to take over the project.
